Farm calendar

Lavender takes a few seasons to settle in. Here's what's happening at the farm between now and opening day, and what each season will look like once the gates open in 2030.

The road to opening day

Dates are plans, and farms run on weather. We're building in three phases. Sign up for updates and you'll hear as each one happens.

  1. Now

    Help shape the farm Open

    Short surveys for visitors, photographers, chefs and local shops. Your answers decide what we grow and offer.

  2. Phase 12027

    Clearing the land and planting the lavender

    The hillside is cleared and prepared, and the first 800 Grosso, Phenomenal, Spanish and French lavender plants go in, in rows that follow the slope.

  3. Phase 22028

    The organic garden, greenhouse and bees

    A deer-fenced organic garden, a greenhouse for starts and an apiary tucked in the shade beside them. The first farm honey comes in.

  4. Phase 32028–29

    The farm building and dinner deck

    A farm shop, workshop space and kitchen for lavender goods, plus a deck for farm-to-table dinners, built while the lavender matures.

  5. 2030

    Open to visitors

    U-pick, photo sessions, workshops, farm-to-table dinners and lavender goods made on the farm.

  6. June 2030

    First June Bloom Day Planned

    A free day to wander the rows in full bloom, with lavender lemonade, iced lavender matcha, lavender honey ice cream and u-pick bouquets.

A year at the farm, once we open

What to expect season by season from 2030 on.

Spring

March to May

  • Garden workshops and plant starts
  • Bees back at work in the canyon
  • First fresh produce from the garden

Bloom

June and July

  • June Bloom Day
  • U-pick bundles and bouquets
  • Photo sessions among the rows
  • Farm-to-table dinners on the deck

Harvest

August to October

  • Drying and bundling lavender
  • Honey harvest
  • Dinners as the evenings cool

Winter

November to February

  • Wreath and gift workshops
  • Lavender goods for the holidays
  • The fields rest
